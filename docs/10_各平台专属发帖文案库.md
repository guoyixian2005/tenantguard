Hey everyone,

Over the past six months, I’ve been running forensic audits on suspected fake paystubs and altered bank statements submitted to mom-and-pop landlords. With eviction memories still fresh and average legal eviction costs hovering around $15,000–$25,000, tenant application fraud is currently at an all-time high.

Between $5 online fake stub generators (ThePayStubs, PaystubMaker) and basic Adobe Acrobat PDF edits, roughly 1 in 5 problematic applicants are now doctoring their income numbers.

Traditional screening reports (TransUnion, SmartMove) only pull past credit bureau debts—they completely ignore whether the uploaded income PDF was generated in an online generator ten minutes prior. 

After dissecting 50+ confirmed fake documents, here are the 4 biggest red flags you should look for before handing over the keys:

---

### 1. The FICA Social Security Math Trap (Statutory 6.2%)
Online fake stub generators are notoriously bad at math. 
Under US federal law, Social Security tax is strictly **6.2%** of gross earnings (up to the annual statutory cap). 
- **The giveaway**: Scammers love round numbers. If gross pay is $4,500, Social Security MUST be exactly **$279.00**. Fake stubs frequently round it to $150.00, $200.00, or use outdated percentage tables.
- **The test**: Grab a calculator and multiply Gross Pay by 0.062. If it’s off by more than 10 cents, you are almost certainly looking at an altered or counterfeit document.

### 2. The Medicare Math Discrepancy (Statutory 1.45%)
Just like Social Security, statutory Medicare deduction is strictly **1.45%** of all gross wages. 
- Real commercial payroll software (ADP, Workday, Paychex, Gusto) never makes arithmetic rounding mistakes on Medicare. 
- If someone claims $6,000 gross monthly pay, Medicare deduction must equal **$87.00**. We routinely catch documents where Medicare is listed as $40 or $50 because the template creator didn't know the exact federal percentage.

### 3. Basic Arithmetic Failure: Gross - Deductions ≠ Net Pay
This sounds obvious, but it catches manual Photoshop fraudsters every single time:
- Applicants often use a PDF editor to change "Net Pay" from $3,000 to $5,000 to meet your 3x rent rule.
- But they completely forget to adjust the Total Deductions column. 
- **The test**: Add up Gross Pay, subtract Total Deductions, and verify if it matches Net Pay down to the penny. In over 30% of modified PDFs, the arithmetic literally does not balance.

### 4. Digital PDF Metadata Footprints
Enterprise payroll platforms generate PDFs using proprietary automated backend servers. Counterfeiters generate them using consumer design tools.
If you open the PDF properties (File > Properties in Acrobat, or Command + I in Mac Preview):
- If the **Producer** or **Creator** says *Canva*, *Adobe Photoshop*, *iLovePDF*, *Sejda*, or *PDFescape*, reject it immediately. No legitimate employer processes payroll through Canva.
- Also, look at the **Modified Date**. If the paystub pay date is March 15th but the PDF Modified Date is October 4th at 11:42 PM, someone altered the file right before applying.
